Termes de cet épisode

Roshi
Japanese honorific for a senior Zen teacher, literally "old teacher"; a title conferred on someone authorized to teach.
Zen
A school of Mahayana Buddhism that developed in China (Chan) and Japan, emphasizing seated meditation (zazen) and direct insight over doctrinal study.
Bodhicitta
Sanskrit, "awakening mind/heart": the aspiration to attain awakening for the sake of ending the suffering of all beings; the core motivation of the Mahayana path.
Bodhisattva
A being who vows to attain awakening in order to liberate all beings, postponing personal liberation for the sake of others.
Rakusu
A small rectangular garment worn around the neck in Zen, a miniature version of the monastic robe (kesa), received at lay or priestly ordination.
Dogen
Eihei Dogen (1200–1253), founder of the Soto school of Zen in Japan and author of the Shobogenzo; the source of the phrase "take the backward step."
Pali Canon
The earliest complete collection of Buddhist scriptures, preserved in the Pali language, the scriptural basis of Theravada Buddhism.
Dharma doors
A traditional expression for the many valid gateways or approaches to practice and awakening; different temperaments require different paths.
Mandala
A circular symbolic diagram of a sacred realm in Indian and Tibetan traditions; used here figuratively for the whole field or territory of suffering one enters.
Upaya
Sanskrit, "skillful means": adapting the teaching to the needs and capacity of the listener. Also the name of Joan Halifax's Zen center in Santa Fe.
Prajna
Sanskrit, "wisdom": the discerning insight into the nature of reality, especially emptiness; paired with compassion (karuna) as the two wings of the path.
Engaged Buddhism
A modern movement, associated with Thich Nhat Hanh and others, applying Buddhist practice directly to social, political, and ecological suffering.
GRACE
A five-step protocol developed by Joan Halifax for cultivating compassion in the moment of encountering suffering: Gather attention, Recall intention, Attune to self and other, Consider what will serve, Engage and End.
Empathic distress
The over-identification with another's suffering to the point of personal overwhelm; distinguished from compassion, which remains grounded and oriented toward helping.
Pathological altruism
Helping behavior that, through imbalance or lack of boundaries, harms the helper, the person helped, or the institution served.
Rohingya
A Muslim ethnic minority of Rakhine State in Myanmar, subjected to mass violence and forced displacement, especially in 2017.
Dolpo
A remote high-altitude region of northwestern Nepal with a Tibetan Buddhist culture, visited by Halifax's Nomads Clinic.
Forest bathing
Shinrin-yoku, a Japanese practice of unhurried immersion in a forest environment for the sake of physical and psychological health.
